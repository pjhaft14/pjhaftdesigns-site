ClubSphere

Connecting a scout’s match notes to a coach’s lineup decisions.

Previously, one scout took paper notes throughout a 90-minute match and used halftime to catch up.
Now, the scout uses ClubSphere to record player ratings in minutes during play.

Research

What the scout records and what the coach needs.

I interviewed a scout and a head coach from an Orlando club. Both interviews shaped the workflows below. Only the scout took part in the live-match pilot.

Design decisions

Designing the handoff between scouting and match planning.

I had three weeks and no live club data, so I focused on the handoff from scouting to match planning. Scheduling, messaging, and payments were outside the scope.

Considered
A department-by-department platform that mirrored how a club is staffed.
Chose
A shared player record. A coach could review a scout’s assessment alongside the latest availability update.

Information architecture

One Squad Hub, seven connected workspaces.

The Squad Hub puts player readiness at the center. Scouting, medical, and training staff would each update the same record from their own workspace.

The solution

Record the assessment, then plan the lineup.

The scout tried the assessment flow at a live match. The remaining screens show the proposed coaching workflow, informed by the coach interview.

Scouting

Fast capture for scouts

I split the long form into Technical, Physical, Mental, and Tactical tabs. Large rating controls put a few attributes within reach at a time.

Squad Readiness

From team status to player clearance

For the coach, the next question is who can play. The Squad Hub flags players with limited availability and links to their profiles.

The profile shows training status, medical restrictions, and dated staff notes so the coach can see who last checked the player and why their status changed.

Match Center

Lineup decisions before kickoff

In Match Center, the coach can move players between the bench and the starting XI while checking fitness, fatigue, and recent minutes.

Player details open beside the formation so the coach can compare an alternative before confirming the squad.

Match Center follow-up

The lineup change reaches the player

Confirming a lineup change sends the player a mobile notification before they arrive at the stadium.

Analytics

From signal to staff decision

Analytics helps staff spot fatigue risk and check the minutes and training status of the players behind it.

Staff can cap a player’s minutes, select an alternative, or move the player into recovery. They review the plan before sharing it.

Results

What one scout could do during a live match.

The scout used an iPad to track five player attributes. I watched whether they could keep up with play and finish the assessment without prompting.

  • 01
    Player ratings took minutes during play, with 10–15 minutes for detailed notes after the match

    On paper, the scout jotted notes throughout the 90-minute match and used halftime to catch up. Looking down to write could mean missing a play. On the iPad, the scout tapped ratings as play unfolded. Once saved, the report synced to the staff’s app without a separate email or attachment.

  • 02
    Large controls held up during live play

    The scout could rate a player one-handed while holding the iPad and watching the pitch.

  • 03
    The scout completed the evaluation independently

    Afterward, the scout rated their confidence between four and five out of five.

Retrospective

Useful player information needs a clear source.

The scout could leave the longer notes until after the final whistle. That made the rating controls the priority during play.

My next test would be with a coach: when two staff updates disagree about a player’s availability, can the coach identify the latest source and decide who starts?
