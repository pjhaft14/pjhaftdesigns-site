Puroresu Project

Making 72 years of Japanese pro wrestling history easier for fans to explore and understand.

I designed and built an interactive archive linking the wrestlers, promotions, eras, and traditions that shaped Japanese pro wrestling. The archive recorded 35,317 website views from July 1 to October 30, 2026.

The problem

From VHS tapes to an interactive archive.

I was about ten when I started buying All Japan Pro Wrestling and FMW matches on VHS from U.S. tape shops and traders. I learned the history one match at a time.

For international fans, that history is scattered across articles, videos, and biographies, many available only in Japanese. A newcomer can find a match and still have no idea how the wrestlers are connected.

I built the archive to give newcomers a way in and longtime fans a way to follow those connections.

Design decisions

Organize the records around the relationships.

A newcomer might arrive without knowing a name to search for. A longtime fan might come looking for a particular match.

Considered
A single searchable archive that exposed every record at the same level.
Chose
A layered entry model, so newcomers get a guided first path while experienced fans can branch into deeper reference material.

Information architecture

Explore by section or follow a curated trail.

The Story introduces the eras and leads into Timeline. Fans who know what they want can go straight to a collection. Archive Trails offer another route. Pick a theme and follow its connections.

Content system

One place to maintain the historical record.

I generate the collections from shared records. Correcting a name, date, or relationship in one place carries the correction across the archive.

The solution

A homepage built from broadcast memory.

Oversized, grainy photographs recall the Japanese wrestling magazines I grew up with.

From the homepage, the Tiger Mask trail connects matches in The Vault with wrestler profiles, lineage, and masks. A fan can start with a familiar face and follow a connection they didn’t know about.

The Story

A guided way into unfamiliar history.

For a reader who wants the historical context first, The Story introduces the eras and leads directly into the Timeline.

Timeline

Historical context became something to browse.

The Timeline places a match or turning point within its era, alongside the promotions and events that shaped it.

Wrestler archive

Profiles became a network, not a stack of biographies.

Readers can find a wrestler by name, promotion, era, gender, or rivalry. A profile opens beside the roster so they can follow an opponent without losing their place.

Each profile brings together the biography, titles, and key matches behind those connections.

Portrait system

Consistent framing makes wrestlers easier to recognize.

Obscured faces and uneven framing made some wrestlers hard to recognize. For several from the 1950s and 1960s, clear portraits were scarce.

Roster portraits were the only photography work where I used AI. I trained the models to preserve each wrestler’s features and kept the framing consistent across the roster.

Connected data

Lineage made the archive visible as a system.

A biography tells Rikidōzan’s story. Lineage shows who trained, influenced, or worked alongside a wrestler.

Selecting Tiger Mask brings his connections to Antonio Inoki and New Japan into focus while the surrounding network stays visible.

Color distinguishes types of relationships. Direction shows how influence moves across generations.

The Vault

A childhood wrestling room, rebuilt as an archive.

On tape, I watched Misawa dethrone Tsuruta and Jushin Liger face Great Sasuke in the 1994 Super J-Cup. I wanted fans to experience that feeling of finding a match worth passing on.

I built The Vault as a room readers can explore, with tapes, posters, and games from my own collection alongside the match archive.

Venues

The places behind the matches.

A match record gives the event a date. The Venues collection gives it a place, from the major arenas to regional halls.

Switching to the map keeps the same filters and shows the distances wrestlers traveled between stops.

Experience system

Familiar controls across different collections.

Search, filters, and detail panels work the same way across collections, so readers can follow a trail without learning new controls at every stop.

Design language

Built from broadcast memory and archival evidence.

The visual language comes from ringside photography, VHS contrast, Japanese magazine type, and television graphics.

On near-black surfaces, white carries the story. Red marks the selected path, a date, or a turning point.

Validation

Check the records, connections, and interface.

  • 01
    Check the historical records and connections

    I checked for duplicate entries, broken references, and relationships that appeared in only one of two linked records.

  • 02
    Check navigation across devices

    I checked broken paths and responsive layouts across mobile, tablet, and desktop, including long filters and detail panels.

  • 03
    Check interaction and motion

    I reviewed touch targets, keyboard focus, reduced-motion behavior, and media-heavy pages as their interface states changed.

I add new records and recheck older links as more sources become available.

Retrospective

Clarity also means showing the limits of the record.

The hardest decisions were often about how much certainty the interface could imply. A clear line or a precise date can look authoritative even when the underlying history is incomplete.

Readers wrote to tell me what they’d discovered. One described following the archive into 1990s All Japan, the same era I first encountered on tape.
