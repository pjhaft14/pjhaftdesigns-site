SugarAI · Mobile CRM · Manufacturing field sales

Keeping customer information up to date without interrupting on-site sales visits.

Reps reported spending 90–120 minutes per customer visit getting their notes into the CRM. In a field test, two reps averaged 15 minutes per visit, dictating updates at customer job sites and syncing them to SugarAI.

Role
Lead UX designer, partnered with a PM and an engineer
Scope
Product strategy, UX, UI
Timeline
About 4.5 months · 2026
Status
In development · slated for Q1 2027

The problem

Customer context was spread across screens.

Sugar mobile carried the desktop CRM onto a phone. To answer a question on the plant floor, reps had to piece together account details across dashboards, records, and forms.

To keep the conversation moving, reps kept notes in spreadsheets, emails, notepads, and on paper. Back at the office, they had to compile and type them again, often in pieces between other tasks. Some forgot, and those visits never made it into the CRM.

Research

Design for the rep standing inside the plant.

I interviewed five sales reps who worked on factory floors, in warehouses, and at sites with poor connectivity. During a visit they needed practical answers about stock, pricing, equipment, and service history.

They were willing to record updates if they could keep talking to the customer.

Design challenge

What does a rep need before, during, and after a visit?

I used the visit as the starting point. A rep needed to know which site was next, answer questions there, and review what would go into the CRM before saving.

AI answers also needed the account, inventory, and service context that reps used to judge them.

Design decisions

Four choices shaped the redesign.

01

Start with Today

Considered
A faster module launcher for accounts, tasks, and opportunities.
Chose
A Today view organized around the next customer visit.

A field rep does not wake up needing Accounts. In interviews, the most important task of their day was preparing for the next customer interaction: where to go, what changed, and what to do when they arrive.

02

Make sites first-class

Considered
Treating every location as detail inside the account record.
Chose
Site-level visit context so reps could prepare for the actual place they were entering.

Manufacturing accounts often span plants, warehouses, HQ offices, and service locations. Each site carries different contacts, risks, and work.

03

Keep AI inside the customer visit

Considered
Making SugarAI the primary surface for every task.
Chose
Put briefings, questions, and dictation inside the active customer visit.
04

Review before saving

Considered
Auto-saving dictated notes directly to the CRM record.
Chose
A review step so reps could trust what changed before committing it.

Messy field input becomes suggested CRM updates the rep can inspect, edit, remove, and save with confidence.

The solution

The first recording follows a complete visit. The next three show what happens when a rep needs a product, an answer about service, or a quick way to dictate a note.

After

Results

What happened when reps took the concept into the field.

Two of the five sales reps I interviewed connected the working concept to their own SugarAI instances and used it at customer job sites.

  • 01
    CRM entry per visit fell from 90–120 minutes to 15 minutes

    They reviewed their dictated notes on site and synced the updates to their own CRM records before returning to the office. The 90–120 minute baseline is the time reps reported spending per visit to compile notes and enter them into the CRM, usually spread across the day.

  • 02
    Visit planning needed to connect to their calendar

    Both reps wanted visits to appear in the calendar they already used. I put personal-calendar sync on the roadmap for the next release.

  • 03
    Reps valued hands-free dictation most

    Voice capture let them record a visit in the car or while walking, without typing or holding a pen.

Retrospective

Hands-free capture mattered more than any screen.

Reps were most enthusiastic about dictation, because it let them capture a visit in the car or while walking. With typing out of the way, reviewing the suggested updates became the main step in CRM entry.

Two reps is a small test, and the baseline is self-reported. Next, I’d measure entry time directly and track how many suggested updates reps keep, edit, or remove.

Offline mode and calendar sync were scoped for later releases, after the planned Q1 2027 launch.
