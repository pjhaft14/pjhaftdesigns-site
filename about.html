About

I design for the
messy middle of
enterprise software.

My work starts where customer data, business rules, automation, and people collide. That is usually where the interesting design problems are hiding.

How I think

Good product design gives people enough context to move forward.

I’m PJ Haft, a product designer working across product strategy, interaction design, research synthesis, prototyping, and design systems, usually on CRM, analytics, dashboards, automation, and AI-assisted workflows.

The moments that interest me most are the ones where a product has to explain itself: what a number means, what changed, what should happen next, or whether a recommendation can be trusted. That is where design starts to matter, because that is where people decide whether the product is helping.

Approach

The principles I keep coming back to.

Clarity

If the system is complicated, the interface has to work even harder to be plain.

Trust

People need to understand what they are seeing before they can act on it.

Craft

Even practical software deserves rhythm, restraint, and care in the details.

Experience

Sixteen years designing the systems people rely on to make decisions.

My experience spans enterprise SaaS, CRM, data integration, analytics, and ecommerce. The hard part is rarely a single screen; it is aligning the system, the workflow, and the people responsible for building it.

That has meant shaping direction with product and engineering, building shared patterns, and carrying ideas from early ambiguity through shipped product.

Beyond work

A few things that keep me curious.

Away from the screen, I’m usually traveling with my wife, hanging with our dog Mylo, checking on bonsai trees, watching Liverpool, or getting pulled back into some corner of Japanese culture and professional wrestling.

I like things with history, ritual, atmosphere, and craft. A good football club, a well-shaped tree, an old wrestling poster, a train station in a city I have never visited before. Different worlds, same pull: small details that make something feel considered instead of merely assembled.

That probably explains why I’m drawn to product work with a lot of moving parts. The structure matters, but so does the feeling of moving through it.
